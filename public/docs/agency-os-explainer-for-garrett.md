# Agency OS: how it works

## In one sentence

Agency OS is a shared workbench for running client work from the first enquiry through delivery, payment, reporting, and review.

## Why it helps

Today, client details, content, decisions, links, and payment notes can end up in separate chats and documents. Agency OS brings the next action and the relevant context together, so work is easier to hand over and less dependent on one person's memory.

## The flow

1. **Track the client.** Move a client from lead and proposal into onboarding, active work, review, case study, or offboarding.
2. **Set up the workspace.** Record the package, scope, goals, assets, approvals, reporting rhythm, and important links using the same checklist each time.
3. **Assign and make the work.** Brief a freelancer by deliverable, with a due date and an internal quality check before the client sees it.
4. **Review together.** Keep the post or deliverable, comments, requested changes, approval, and activity history in one place.
5. **Plan and report.** Use the calendar and idea bank for upcoming work. Collect monthly results, progress notes, wins, issues, and next actions in Updates.
6. **Track the money.** Record client invoices and receipts, operating expenses, and freelancer costs and payouts. Revolut can remain the place money moves; the OS is the operations record that helps reconcile it.
7. **Learn and repeat.** Ask for a review around weeks 4–6, capture results and permissioned examples, then use the same process for the next client.

## What each area is for

- **Home:** the day's decisions and important work in motion.
- **Projects:** each client's scope, work, owner, and current stage.
- **Review:** deliverables that need an internal or client decision.
- **Calendar:** what is planned, scheduled, or published and when.
- **Idea bank:** client-specific ideas, references, research, and inspiration.
- **Updates:** weekly freelancer updates and monthly results.
- **Client portal:** the client-facing workspace for content, approvals, resources, and onboarding.
- **Financials:** money expected, received, owed, and paid, with operating costs and freelancer payout timing.
- **Operations:** the agency's shared SOPs, handover checklists, client pipeline, and freelancer directory.

## Access and responsibility

Client social accounts should be owned by the client or agency and shared through named platform permissions where possible. Freelancers receive only the access needed for their assigned work; they do not receive client passwords. Passwords and recovery codes belong in the agency's approved password manager. Freelancer agreements and NDA status should be recorded before work begins.

## What it is not

Agency OS is not the bank, accounting system, password vault, or social publishing platform. It records the workflow and payment status so the team can coordinate and reconcile those tools. The current public link is a visual prototype: edits are temporary, and its role switcher does not secure data. Real client or freelancer information should only go into a private, authenticated version after access controls and storage are connected.

## What a new operator should do first

Open Operations, follow the client onboarding SOP, confirm the owner and next action for each client, then work the review queue and payment checklist. When something happens, update the matching project, task, report, or payment record so the whole team sees the same status.
